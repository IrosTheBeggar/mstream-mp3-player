# Text in other languages

"Multi-language" is two jobs on the Core2, and both rest on the same text
code:

1. **Showing names in any script**: titles, artists, albums and folders in
   Greek, Cyrillic, Japanese, Korean, Chinese and the rest, as the library
   holds them.
2. **Translating the UI**: menus, toasts and dialogs in other languages.

This is the plan in five phases. Names come first (phases 0-2): every
library has them, and a UI language in another script needs phase 1 and
2's fonts anyway. Phase 0's width-test fix also guards the translations
later. Phase 4 is optional.

Status: **phase 0 is built** (October 2026, for the release after v0.8.0):
its host tests pass and both firmware builds pass their guards; its device
checks are [below](#device-checks). **Phase 1's fonts are built** too
(October 2026): Greek, Cyrillic and more Latin in the four UI fonts, the
rows as high as before; its device checks are
[in its section](#device-checks-1). **Phase 2a is built** (October 2026):
10,557 CJK and Hangul glyphs at 16 px in flash, drawn behind the fonts in
every role, the kana and Hangul in the order, and line breaks between
Chinese and Japanese characters; its device checks are
[in its section](#device-checks-2). **Phase 3's groundwork is built**
(October 2026): every UI text in `i18n/en.json`, drawn through
`uistr::tr()` (the English as before); the ten languages in the
firmware, the language setting (Output > Language, and a fresh unit's
first screen), "Various Artists" drawn in the UI's language, and a width
test for every language. **The nine translations are in** (October 2026):
every text in de, es, fr, it, ja, pl, pt, ru and zh, machine translations
from the mStream apps' words and Fable agents
([the translations](#the-translations)), not yet reviewed by native
speakers. The layout fixes for the rooms still too narrow (29) and the
device checks are [still to do](#still-to-do-in-phase-3). Phase 1's order and rail items,
phase 2b and phase 4 are plans, with the choices in
[Decisions still open](#decisions-still-open).

## The text path, and what never changes

- **Tags in:** ID3v2 text in any of its encodings, Vorbis comments, Opus
  and APE as UTF-8. Latin-1 is ISO-8859-1, byte for byte: a cp1252 byte
  0x80-0x9F stays U+0080-009F in the record ([METADATA.md](METADATA.md)
  5.2). A legacy code page in a Latin-1 frame (cp1251, GBK) arrives as
  Latin-1 mojibake; telling them apart would be a contract change on both
  sides.
- **What is stored is never normalised:** a record keeps its values as
  decoded (control characters as spaces, each field cut to 255 bytes);
  paths are the card's bytes, and the path hashes are over them (NFC and
  NFD hash differently); the index's track name is what the file is
  opened by, and a title is often a slice of it ([METADATA.md](METADATA.md)
  2.3.3, 2.3.6, 2.8.5; `lib/core/CardContract.h`). Every fold and
  composition below is made where a name is drawn, compared or sorted,
  never written back.
- **library.idx** keeps the names as UTF-8 and the order computed when it
  is built (`textfold::compare`), with 27 rail buckets (`#`, A-Z) in its
  header. An order change bumps `LibraryIndex::kRulesVersion`, a bucket
  change the file's version: either way the index rebuilds once. On a card
  that has records it builds from them, with no walk and no scan; a card
  with none (a card-reader card before its scan has written the device's
  `tags.bin`) walks `/music` once instead, as for any Outdated index
  ([METADATA.md](METADATA.md) 3.4.3). Rules 3 came with phase 0, 4 with
  phase 2.
- **The fonts:** four DejaVu Sans VLW fonts in flash (Sans 16 and 13, Bold
  16 and 22), 734 glyphs each since phase 1: ASCII, Latin-1, Latin
  Extended-A, Romanian's and Pinyin's letters, Greek, Cyrillic, the
  typographic punctuation, € and ™ (`tools/vlw_font.py`; 546,165 B of
  flash; M5GFX keeps 9 B a glyph of tables in PSRAM). Their line heights
  are 19, 17, 19 and 27 px, as with Latin alone: the generator fits the
  few taller glyphs to the line. Behind them since phase 2, a glyph store
  of 10,557 CJK and Hangul glyphs at 16 px (`lib/core/CjkGlyphs16.cpp`,
  1.05 MB of flash, read where it lies). A glyph neither has is folded by
  TextFit before drawing ('?' when nothing better), and measured as the
  font's space width (5 px in Sans 16).
- **Drawing:** one glyph per code point, left to right: no shaping,
  kerning, bidi or grapheme clusters. Lines break after a space, and
  between Chinese and Japanese characters (phase 2), else mid-word.
- **The identity key** (`namekey`, [METADATA.md](METADATA.md) 5.4) is
  already right for every script: Unicode's full lowercase with
  Final_Sigma. It names the votes and AutoDJ's keys; it doesn't order.

### How it is built

Phase 0 is six items; its commits name them by these numbers.

1. **The folds** (`lib/core/TextFold`, Full mode): U+0080-009F go through
   `textfold::fromC1()` first (the five cp1252 leaves undefined stay `?`);
   ˆ and ˜ fold to ^ and ~; fullwidth ASCII is arithmetic; Latin
   Extended-B and Extended Additional come from generated tables
   (`kLatinExtB`, `kLatinExtAdditional`, and `kFolds` for two letters and
   the IPA partners). A letter folds to its canonical decomposition's base
   letter, else its compatibility decomposition's letters, else the letter
   its Unicode name is built on ("B WITH STROKE", "OPEN O", "TURNED E", "DB
   DIGRAPH"), else a short explicit list (Ə as E, ẞ as SS, ezh as Z, wynn as
   W), else its other case's fold (ǯ as z, by Ʒ's Z; Ƕ hwair as HV, by
   ƕ's), so a capital and its small letter always fold alike (the
   generator checks every pair). The rest of the two blocks (ƍ, Ʃ, Ȝ, the
   tone letters and the clicks) stays `?`. Combining marks, variation
   selectors (VS1-16, the ideographic VS17-256, Mongolian's) and tag
   characters are dropped. Punctuation mode, which only the font probe
   uses, folds just cp1252's punctuation of U+0080-009F.
2. **Composition** (`textfold::Composer`): a text's code points with each
   combining mark composed into the letter before it as NFC does, by NFC's
   own primary composites for Latin, Greek, Cyrillic and kana (839 pairs, 31
   marks: `kPairs`), and Hangul's jamo by arithmetic, the marks a letter
   can't take left after it in canonical order. For NFD text and for NFC
   text in those scripts the output is NFC's. Not done: the singletons NFC
   maps alone (the Greek oxia letters to their tonos twins, U+037E to `;`),
   and a mark after a precomposed letter that NFC would sort before the
   letter's own marks (ǖ + U+0323): both are left as they are. A run of more
   than 16 marks isn't composed. Other scripts' compositions aren't made
   either (Arabic's hamza and madda letters, Devanagari's nukta letters,
   the Indic two-part vowel signs): there an NFD and an NFC spelling still
   sort apart and don't match. TextFit composes before it looks for glyphs
   (so the fonts' own é is drawn), and the order and the name matching
   compose before they fold. Nothing else does.
3. **The order** (`textfold::compare`, `compareSorted`, `railKey`,
   `secondKey`): a text is walked a unit at a time; a unit is a character of
   the Full folding, lower-cased, or a letter the folding can't spell, as
   its script and its Unicode lowercase (namekey's tables; ς as σ). Where
   two names first differ the units rank: other characters, digits, the
   other scripts' letters (in `textfold::Script`'s order: Latin with no
   fold, such as IPA's, Greek, Cyrillic, Armenian, Hebrew, Arabic, Thai,
   Hangul, Hiragana, Katakana, Han, then any other), the ASCII letters. So
   the other scripts stay under the rail's `#`, after the digits, and the
   rail's buckets stay in order. A script is a code point's block, from a
   curated table (`scriptOf()`), not Unicode's Script property; a block's
   own punctuation counts as letters, except the few marks that split
   names (the Greek question mark and ano teleia, the katakana middle dot).
   The CJK symbols block is the other way round: punctuation, but for its
   letters (々 〆 〇, the Hangzhou numerals, 〻 and 〼 as Han; the vertical
   kana repeat marks as hiragana). The order is still a total one (ties
   fall to the bytes), and a compare still holds no buffer: two cursors of
   about 30 B each.

   **The order of Latin names doesn't change**, apart from the folds above:
   test_text_fold sorts N11's synthetic card's names (4,588 of them, from
   `tools/synthcard.py`'s plan; `test/fixtures/synthcard/names.py`), with
   Latin-1, Extended-A and typographic variants of every seventh, by
   c97ff3c's order (a frozen copy in the test) and by today's: the names
   the old folding could spell come out in the same order, alone and among
   the others, with the same rail letters.
4. **Name matching** (`textfold::sameName`, `startsWithName`): two names
   are the same when their letters and digits are, in the order's units,
   so a letter in another script counts by its lowercase (Кино is КИНО, not
   Kino). Before, a name with no Latin letter or digit matched nothing.
   This is the gate of the artist election
   (`LibraryIndex::closeArtistRun()`: the folder takes its tags' spelling
   and ARTISTSORT when they match) and of TrackName's artist drop
   (`afterArtist()`, and the `Artist - NN - Title` shape); LibraryIndex and
   TrackName are its only callers. It compares the names' letters and
   digits, not whole nameKeys, so it is looser than nameKey equality: case
   and accents fold, one leading "The" drops, and the punctuation a FAT
   name can't hold (`/`, `:`, `?`...) doesn't count (`Кино` and `Ки-но`
   match, though their nameKeys differ). And it is stricter than before
   for names with few Latin letters: `Би-2` and `Ая-2`, alike before by
   their 2, now differ. A name with no letter or digit at all still
   matches nothing.
5. **The width tests** (test_ui_library): `fits()` (about 200 calls over
   the UI's texts) also asserts `hasAll()`, and the host's `Vlw::width()`
   counts a missing glyph as M5GFX's space width (the larger of the font's
   size and its ascent plus descent, times 2/7: 5 px in Sans 16, 4 in Sans
   13), as `ui/Fonts` does. No text needed a change.
   `test_names_drawn_with_the_fonts` draws names through TextFit with the
   real fonts: cp1252 bytes, NFD, the folds, the cut.
6. **library.idx rules 3** (`kRulesVersion` 2 to 3, once for all of it):
   the first boot of this firmware finds the index Outdated and rebuilds it
   once. On a card that has records it builds from them, as after any
   rules change (no walk, no scan); a card with none (a card-reader card
   before its scan has written the device's `tags.bin`) walks `/music`
   once instead, as for any Outdated index. The boots after load it.

**The tables** (items 1 and 2) are made by `tools/gen_text_tables.py` from
Python's unicodedata (`--check` says whether the files are current):
`lib/core/TextFoldTables.cpp` and `test/test_text_fold/compose_cases.h`,
600 random strings in the tables' range (NFD text, NFC text, letters with
up to four marks in any order) with unicodedata's NFC of each.
test_text_fold composes each, and recomputes the generator's digest of
every table entry through TextFold's own functions.

### The line heights

M5GFX's `VLWfont::loadFont` makes a font's line height its glyphs'
tallest ascent plus their deepest descent (of ASCII and everything above
U+00FF; `Fonts::height()` is that). As DejaVu draws them, the new glyphs
would take the rows from 19, 17, 19 and 27 px to 21, 18, 22 and 31 px
(MEASURED: the same fonts, unfitted), and every page's layout with them.

So the generator fits a glyph that reaches above the font's ascent or
below its descent (`fit_to_line()`): it takes rows from the glyph's marks
and the gaps around them, never from its letter, each step the first of

1. a blank row at the glyph's edge;
2. a second blank row between the marks and the letter;
3. a row of a mark 3 rows tall or more: the row inside it nearest the
   mean of its two neighbours goes (a 3-row mark's middle row), so the
   rows left keep their shapes (merged, a tilde's rows made a macron's
   bar and a caron's a breve's cup: found in review);
4. a blank row between two marks;
5. the last blank row between the marks and the letter;
6. a row of any mark (its two rows most alike merge, the darker pixel
   kept).

So a mark stays apart from its letter, and keeps two rows (an acute isn't
a grave), as long as there is room. The glyphs fitted, all of them over
the ascent (none is under the descent):

| Font | Fitted | Over by |
|---|---|---|
| Sans 16 | Ă Ӑ ΐ ΰ ΅ Ǖ; Ǘ Ǚ Ǜ | 1 px; 2 px |
| Sans 13 | ĺ Ǖ Ǘ Ǚ Ǜ | 1 px |
| Bold 16 | Ă Ӑ Ĩ Ũ Ѯ ΐ ΰ ΅ Ǖ Ã Ñ Õ; Ǘ Ǚ Ǜ | 1 px; 3 px |
| Bold 22 | ΐ ΰ ΅; Ǖ; Ǘ Ǚ Ǜ | 1 px; 2 px; 4 px |

At 16 px, Ǘ's diaeresis rests on the U, one row high, under a two-row
acute; at 22 px both marks keep two rows and the gap. Bold 16's
three-row tildes (Ã Ñ Õ Ĩ Ũ) lose their middle row and keep a two-row
wave, not Ā's bar, and Bold 22's Ǚ keeps its caron's V
(`tools/test_vlw_font.py` checks both on the TTFs). The capitals one
would expect to be tall (Й Ё Ά Έ Ή Ί Ό Ύ Ώ Ǎ) fit as DejaVu draws them.
Latin-1's Ã Ñ Õ in Bold 16 were 1 px over the line since the first fonts
(M5GFX doesn't count Latin-1, so it drew them 1 px into the row above, or
cut them at a sprite's edge); they fit now too, and are the only glyphs of
3a1835a's 256 that changed.

`test_fonts_keep_their_line_heights` computes M5GFX's rule on the
generated arrays: each font's ascent, descent, baseline and line height
are 3a1835a's, and no glyph, Latin-1's included, leaves the line (with the
fitting off, it fails). The generator checks the same rule as it writes,
and `python tools/vlw_font.py --check` says whether `src/ui/VlwFonts.cpp`
is what it makes now (with DejaVu 2.37, and Pillow 12.2 with its FreeType
2.14: another FreeType may hint a pixel differently). The tool reads each
font's cmap for the characters it has, since Pillow draws a missing one as
the font's box.

### How it is built

Phase 2 is seven items; its commits name them by these numbers.

1. **A store format:** [below](#the-glyph-store), built first with
   items 2 and 3 (6965533).
2. **Its generator** (`tools/glyph_store.py`).
3. **Its reader** (`lib/core/GlyphStore`).
4. **The fonts consult it** (`ui/Fonts`):
   - **TextFit's view of a font** (`Fonts::fit()`): a code point's width
     and whether there is a glyph come from the VLW font first, then the
     store (`hasGlyph()`, `widthOf()`), so TextFit keeps CJK and Hangul as
     they are, measures them by their own advance, and folds only what
     neither has.
   - **What a page measures** (`Fonts::width()`, and `cutPathLeft()` for
     the Folders header and Go to folder): a stored text as it will be
     drawn, composed and folded first (`textfit::drawnWidth()`, no
     buffer), so a halfwidth katakana or NFD Hangul name is as wide as its
     16 px glyphs, not a row of 4-5 px blanks for code points no font has.
     Measured raw, `/music/ｱﾆﾒ/ｻｳﾝﾄﾞﾄﾗｯｸ` was 99 px in Small against 227
     drawn: the path was kept whole and its end cut, where its fullwidth
     twin keeps `…/アニメ/サウンドトラック` (MEASURED with the firmware's
     `Fonts.cpp` on the PC).
   - **Drawing:** a fitted line with no store glyph is drawn by M5GFX's
     `drawString()`, as before, so Latin, Greek and Cyrillic rows don't
     change by a pixel. The store holds nothing TextFold folds to ASCII
     (a test checks), so a Latin name with a sign such as − (U+2212,
     JIS X 0208's minus, folded to `-`) stays such a row. A line with a
     store glyph is drawn by `Fonts::drawMixed()` a glyph at a time: each
     VLW glyph by M5GFX's own `VLWfont::drawChar()`, the call
     `drawString()` makes, with the same fill tracking (`filled_x`); each
     store glyph by `drawGlyph()`. M5GFX's
     emoji callback (`setEmojiCallback()`) was the other way, and was
     left after reading M5GFX 0.2.30's `LGFXBase::draw_string()` and
     `text_width()`: the callback gets the line's top and the font's
     height, not the role's baseline, so it would need a "current draw"
     set by `Fonts::draw()` and installed on every sprite and the panel;
     M5GFX fills no background behind its glyph and doesn't move its
     fill mark; a return of 0 makes M5GFX draw its missing-glyph box
     (`drawCharDummy()`), so a glyph that doesn't advance can't be drawn;
     and `textWidth()` counts a callback glyph as wide as the line is
     tall, so the alignment would be ours anyway.
   - **Alignment:** `drawMixed()` measures the line as M5GFX's
     `text_width()` does (to the last glyph's ink or advance, the first
     glyph's ink left of the pen included), a store glyph by its own box
     and advance, and centres or right-aligns on that; the line's top is
     `y - lineHeight / 2`, as `drawString()`'s middle datums put it. It
     returns the width drawn, as `drawString()` does.
   - **A store glyph** (`drawGlyph()`): with a background (bg != fg), the
     glyph's cell first, the line's whole height from where the last
     glyph's fill stopped to its advance, as M5GFX fills a VLW glyph's;
     then its coverage (4 bits, times 17), fg blended over bg as M5GFX
     blends a VLW pixel (`(fg * p + bg * (257 - p)) >> 8`, p = 1 +
     coverage), composed in a box on the stack and pushed in one
     `pushImage()`. With no background (bg == fg, a sprite), and where a
     glyph reaches back over the one before it, the box is read back
     (`readRect()`), blended and pushed. A target that can't be read gets
     M5GFX's own fallback, the glyph blended with its base colour a pixel
     at a time. The box is cut to the clip rectangle.
   - **RGB565 byte order:** the box is M5GFX's `swap565_t`, the type of a
     16-bit sprite's pixels and the panel's (color depth `rgb565_2Byte`:
     RGB565 with its bytes swapped, as the panel takes them), so
     `readRect()` and `pushImage()` copy it with no conversion, and the
     colours are made by `swap565_t`'s own constructor and read by its
     `R8()`/`G8()`/`B8()`, never by hand. A plain `uint16_t*` would have
     meant `swap565_t` too (M5GFX's default, `setSwapBytes(false)`), and
     `rgb565_t` the unswapped order. Checked without a device: the
     firmware's `src/ui/Fonts.cpp`, unchanged, built on the PC against
     M5GFX 0.2.30 itself (LovyanGFX's desktop back end, no window) and
     drawing into a real 16-bit `LGFX_Sprite`. A store glyph's fully
     covered pixel holds the same two bytes as a VLW glyph's that M5GFX
     drew (F7 9F for the text colour 0xF79F), and the background's are
     unchanged; and every preview (Now Playing, the Library's rows, the
     Queue's) drawn so is the same, pixel for pixel, as an independent
     renderer of the rules above in Python. The panel's own byte order is
     a device check.
   - **Where a glyph sits:** every role draws CJK at 16 px (bold in Bold
     and Title, below), on its own baseline. The store's em box is 14 px
     above the baseline and 2 below (`GlyphStore::baselineIn()`): the role's
     baseline when the em box fits under the row's top, else lower. Then
     `glyphstore::place()` moves a glyph whose ink reaches above the row
     down into it, only as far as it must, and leaves out the rows past
     the row's bottom. MEASURED over all 10,557 glyphs
     (test_ui_library's `test_store_glyphs_sit_in_the_rows`):

     | Role | Row, DejaVu's baseline | CJK baseline | Moved down | Cut |

     | Body, Sans 16 | 19 px, 15 | 15 | 1 (Å U+212B, 1 px) | 0 |

     and Title roles draw its glyphs a synthetic bold, to sit beside
     DejaVu Sans Bold. Each row's 4-bit coverage is spread a pixel to the
     right at half strength, a'(x) = min(15, a(x) + a(x-1) / 2), from the
     values as they were (`glyphstore::embolden()`: in place, right to
     left). The box grows by a column and the advance by a pixel
     (`glyphstore::bold()`; a mark's 0 advance stays 0), and everything
     that measures in those roles measures so: TextFit's width
     (`Fonts::fit()`), `Fonts::width()`, `drawMixed()`'s alignment, the
     cuts and the wraps. Body and Small draw as before. Chosen from four
     rules, each drawn by the firmware's own `Fonts.cpp` on the PC (Now
     Playing, the Queue's rows, dense Han and Hangul) and measured over
     the whole store:

     | Rule | a'(x) | Han's ink | Han's 1 px gaps kept | Han's grey 3 px stems |
     |---|---|---:|---:|---:|
     | none | a(x) | x1.00 | 100 % | 2.8 % |
     | max | max(a(x), a(x-1)) | x1.38 | 66 % | 23.5 % |
     | add (FreeType's `FT_Bitmap_Embolden`) | min(15, a(x) + a(x-1)) | x1.53 | 65 % | 1.4 % |
     | **half** (built) | min(15, a(x) + a(x-1) / 2) | x1.30 | 76 % | 1.7 % |
     | max, gaps kept | max, but half the left value in a 1 px gap | x1.34 | 78 % | 23.5 % |

     (A gap is kept when its darkest pixel is 5/15 or less, with ink of
     8/15 or more each side of it in the row: 184,702 of them in the
     regular Han. A grey stem is a run of ink 3 px or wider with no pixel
     of 12/15 or more.) The max rules copy a stem's anti-aliased edge
     sideways, so a stem that sits across two pixels becomes a dim 3 px
     band (피's ㅣ, the middle stroke of 東); the add rules keep a bright
     core. The full add closes the 1 px gaps: 鬱 響 纏 become blots, ㅐ
     and ㅔ one thick stem (대 like 다), ㅃ a block. Half the add leaves

     weight: a semi-bold, x1.30 the regular's ink, where DejaVu Sans Bold
     has x1.81 DejaVu Sans's, since only the vertical strokes grow (a
     horizontal one stays 1 px). A bold that also grows a pixel up was
     closer on simple glyphs (東 京 カタカナ) but turned dense Han to mush,

     advance). Its cost in a line: Now Playing's 190 px holds 11 Han as

   two characters of which one is Han, kana, CJK punctuation or a
   fullwidth form (Latin beside them included: `Tokyo東京` / `Jihen事変`),
   but never before a closing mark (、。，．・：；？！）」』】〕〉》〙〗, the
   closing quotes ” ’ 〟 〞 that Chinese text uses, and the ASCII ones), an
   ellipsis (… ‥), 〜, a small kana, ー, 々, ゝ ゞ ヽ ヾ or ゛ ゜, nor after an
   opening mark (（「『【〔〈《〘〖, “ ‘ 〝 and `( [ {`). These are the marks
   it knows: another closing or opening sign may end or start a line
   beside CJK. Hangul breaks at its spaces, and inside a word only when
   the word alone is wider than the line (as any word). The ideographic
   space U+3000 (16 px from the store) is a break, dropped at a line's end
   and start. The last line is ellipsised as before. A cut, there and in
   `fit()`, never falls inside a UTF-8 sequence or between a letter and a
   combining mark, whether the width or the buffer makes it (a test cuts
   inside every pair of a kana and its 0 px mark). Two fixes on the
   way: the search for the longest line that fits stopped a character
   short when its midpoint fell inside a multi-byte character (every CJK
   line; Latin-1 sometimes); and a kana voicing mark the composer can't
   put on its letter (ア + U+3099: there is no such kana) draws as the
   spacing ゛ or ゜, since the combining mark, drawn over the letter, sits on
   its strokes at 16 px.
7. **2a:** the built-in store in the app slot, one size, its index in
   flash. **2b**, the per-library store on the card: not built.

### The glyph store

- **What it holds:** `lib/core/CjkGlyphs16.cpp`, made by
  `tools/glyph_store.py` from Noto Sans CJK Regular 2.004, its Japanese
  face (SIL OFL 1.1: the data stays under that licence;
  THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md). KS X 1001's 2,350 Hangul syllables and the 94
  compatibility jamo; JIS X 0208 whole (6,355 kanji, its kana and
  symbols, and the cells Windows' cp932 spells otherwise, such as ∥);
  GB2312's 3,755 level 1 hanzi; the Hiragana, Katakana and Katakana
  Phonetic Extensions blocks; CJK Symbols and Punctuation; the fullwidth
  signs TextFold doesn't fold (｟ ｠ ￠-￦ ￩-￮). Less what the VLW fonts
  draw (DejaVu wins: Latin-1, Greek, Cyrillic, the punctuation), the
  fullwidth ASCII, the minus sign − and the halfwidth katakana TextFold
  folds first (to ASCII, so a Latin name with − stays DejaVu's; to the
  fullwidth kana), and seven glyphs whose ink reaches more than 2 px past
  the em box: the marks only vertical text uses (〪-〭, 〱 〲) and ￨.
  10,557 glyphs: 7,741 Han, 2,350 Hangul syllables. The face has no ≒
  (U+2252).
- **The UI's own characters:** every set also takes the characters the
  UI's catalogues use (`--chars-from`, `i18n/` by default: every text of
  `i18n/*.json`), so a translation's hanzi outside the set (锂, a GB2312
  level 2 one) is drawn once the store is made again with the font, and
  the file's header names what the catalogues added. Until then the
  catalogue's check refuses the text and names the code point (phase 3's
  generator, which CI runs as `--check`), so a translation can't lose a
  glyph unnoticed. Today's catalogues add nothing: the store is pan's.
  With phase 3's ten catalogues (MEASURED: `--check` with the font, its
  SHA-256 the header's, says the file is current) Japanese uses 211 Han,
  all JIS X 0208's, 108 kana and 、。々「」; Chinese 433 Han, all GB2312
  level 1's, and 、。.
- **One size for every role:** 16 px to the em. The em box is 14 px above
  the baseline and 2 below; the ink reaches 16 above (Å U+212B) and 3
  below (hooks, box drawing). Han and kana advance 16 px, Hangul 15 (the
  font's narrower Hangul).
- **The format** (the tool's docstring has it whole): a header (the
  pixel size, the em box and how far the ink reaches, the largest box, the
  counts), then sections by tag: the pages (256 code points each) that
  have glyphs; for each page a presence bitmap, its first glyph, where its
  bitmaps start, and the glyphs before each word of the bitmap; for each
  glyph 4 B of metrics (the box, its place from the pen and the baseline,
  the advance) and a 2 B offset; the bitmaps. Code points are 32 bits,
  and a reader skips a section it doesn't know, so a second size, or a
  per-library store on the card (2b), is another store in the same format.
- **The bitmaps:** each glyph's tight box at 4 bits, coded as runs of 0
  and of 15 (the other values a nibble each): 17.9 % smaller than raw over
  the set (1,028,108 B against 1,252,173 B) for a decoder of twenty
  lines. The index is 70 KB of the store (6 B a glyph).
- **Reading it** (`glyphstore::Store`): a binary search over the 135
  pages, one word of the page's bitmap and a bit count, the store read
  where it lies in flash: no heap, no PSRAM, nothing at boot. A lookup
  takes 60 ns on the host (MEASURED; ESTIMATED 1-2 µs on the ESP32 with
  the flash cache warm). A lookup reads only the page tables (6.5 KB), so
  `contains()` stays in the cache; `advance()` reads one more word.
  `decode()` writes a glyph's coverage, 0-15 or 0-255, into the caller's
  buffer.
- **Checks:** `python tools/glyph_store.py --font <the TTC> --check` says
  whether the file is what the font makes (its header names the font
  file, its version and SHA-256, and the FreeType that drew it);
  test_glyph_store reads every glyph back through the reader against the
  generator's digest, and checks the set and that nothing DejaVu draws is
  in it.

### The catalogue

`i18n/<tag>.json`, one file a language, JSON in the shape of the mobile
app's ARB files: a message by id (`"coach.title": "The three red dots"`),
and in `en.json` after each, `"@coach.title"`, what a translator needs:

- its context, its room (the font: Body, Small, Bold or Title; the
  pixels; the lines a layout places one by one; `else`, the smaller font
  the code falls back to; `wrap` for a dialog's body the code wraps;
  `toast` for a note, one Body line or split at its first `": "`; a list
  where the text is drawn in several places; `optional` where the code
  draws it only when it fits, another text or nothing otherwise: a tab's
  label, the toast's buttons, their icons);
- its placeholders, `{0}` to `{3}`, each with what fills it at worst (a
  count, written grouped or as digits; a decimal; a name; another
  message) and, in `or`, the other messages that may fill it (the
  calibration's A hint holds any of six verbs, a skipped track's note any
  of the 19 reasons, Device info's storage "SD" or the flash's
  word), and for a plural message the one that is its count;
- `bytes`: the smallest buffer the text goes through on the device,
  filled, its NUL in it (a toast's 128, the sleep timer's row 48, a sheet
  row's 64, a percentage's 16: 332 messages, from an audit of every call
  site), and `line_bytes` where the code copies it a line at a time (the
  coach cards', the empty states', 20 messages); a text that reaches no
  buffer (drawn from `tr()` as it is) has none;
- `maybe` for the technical words a language may keep (CPU, PSRAM, BPM,
  the reset reasons, the units' formats) and `dev` for what only a
  developer sees (6 texts); `overflow` for a text too wide in English
  already (none since [the layout fixes](#the-layout-fixes); there were 9).

A translation holds only its messages, its `@@autonym` (the language's
name in itself, as the generator has it) and an `@@note`. Its
`"@<id>"` may hold a `note`, and `"overflow": true` for a text that is
too wide in that language until its room is fixed (the width test lets
it be, and fails the mark once it fits). JSON because Python's standard
library reads it (the generators need nothing else), it is the mobile
app's format (its translations map onto ids), it diffs a line a message,
and a file that doesn't parse fails loudly; TOML needs Python 3.11 and has
no writer, YAML a library. `i18n/README.md` is the translators' guide.

**Ids** are `<screen>.<snake_case>` (`library.tracks_n`,
`dialog.lost_title`), stable once given; the C++ name is the id with `_`
for `.` (`S::library_tracks_n`).

**Plurals** are objects of forms by CLDR category: one and other in
English. The generator holds each language to CLDR 46's categories for
whole numbers: one, other (en, de); one, many, other (es, fr, it, pt);
one, few, many, other (pl, ru); other (ja, zh). The ones no count here
reaches are optional: many in es, fr, it and pt (a million's multiples),
other in pl and ru (fractions); "other" stands in for a form left out. A
form may say the count in words ("Added") only where its category is the
number 1 alone (en, de, es, it, pl one); fr's and pt's one is 0 and 1,
ru's 1, 21, 31: those say `{0}`. There are no explicit `=0` forms: where
English says "no tracks", the code picks another message
(`library_row.empty`).

**Line breaks.** A sentence the layout draws over two fixed lines (the
coach cards, the empty states' lines, the calibration's hints, the
Bluetooth card's hints) is one message with `\n` where the line breaks:
the code draws each line where it drew the two constants, so English is
pixel for pixel as before, and a translator breaks where the language
reads well (the width test measures each line). A dialog's body is
wrapped by the code (`textfit::wrap`) as before.

**What stays English** (in `UiText.h`): the identify banner, "This one"
(the terminal player's MP3 tab quotes it in English), and BoardGuard's
screen (Font2, ASCII). The console and the logs stay English, but the
log lines that quote what the screen shows quote it in the UI's
language: the card's message in `[storage]`, the setting labels in
`[screen]` and `[power]`, Device info's `[diag]` lines, a toast's text
(`[ui] toast:`), the sleep timer's row (`[sleep]`, the playback menu's
line), the Queue's summary in the `ui` command. What names the index's
or the code's own things stays English whatever the language: the tag
console's dump of a track (its album's line as `library.idx` holds it,
"Various Artists"; "(loose tracks)"), the Library's segments in the `ui`
command and its `[ui] library:` line. Left in code, as numbers and names:
the times ("3:05", "--:--"), the dot " · ", the badge's "99+", the
codecs' badges, the sleep pills' bare minutes, the version line, the
dBm range, the repository's name, and the Bluetooth card's codec ("SBC"
and its rate: BtSink keeps the rate in Hz, and the card writes it as a
skipped track's note does, `format.khz` with the language's decimal mark:
"SBC 44,1 kHz" in German; `btDetailText()`).

### The generator

`tools/gen_ui_strings.py` (standard library only) checks the catalogue
and writes three files, `--check` in CI:

- `lib/core/UiStringIds.h`: `enum class uistr::S` (a value a message, in
  `en.json`'s order, its English in a comment) and `enum class Lang` (the
  ten languages, en first, whether or not their catalogue is there yet;
  `EnXA` where `UISTR_PSEUDO` is defined).
- `lib/core/UiStringsData.cpp`: per language one blob of UTF-8 (each
  form NUL-terminated, a plural's forms one after the other in its
  categories' order, the same text once) and a `uint16_t` offset an id
  (0xFFFF: not translated, English's is used); a bit an id for the
  plural ones. Const, in flash. A language with no catalogue has no blob
  and no offsets (16 B of table: its tag and its name).
- `test/test_ui_strings/catalogue.h`: the English and each room, which
  languages have a catalogue and what each marks `overflow`, for the
  width tests (host only).

It refuses: a file that isn't UTF-8 (named), an id English lacks, a
plural given as a string or the other way round, a category the language
hasn't or a needed one missing, placeholders other than English's, more
lines than the room, a stray brace, a control character, a lone surrogate
("\ud800"), an `@@autonym` other than the language's name in itself; a
text with no placeholders longer than its `bytes` (or a line than its
`line_bytes`); and a character the device can't draw as itself, every
one in a file named at once with its code point and the ids that use it:
one the VLW fonts (read from `src/ui/VlwFonts.cpp`) and the CJK store
(read from `lib/core/CjkGlyphs16.cpp`) both lack, or one TextFold always
folds, so drawn as another: the fullwidth forms U+FF01-FF5E (Chinese and
Japanese text's ，：（）！？ are drawn as their ASCII: the file writes
those, or the store's 、。「」), the halfwidth katakana, the minus sign
U+2212. In `en.json` it also refuses a message without its context or
room, placeholders it doesn't describe, and an `or` that names no
message. `--report` counts each language's messages, `--dump <tag>`
lists them (`en-XA` too). `tools/test_gen_ui_strings.py` tests the
checks, the pseudo-locale and the tables' C literal read back.

### The runtime

`lib/core/UiStrings` (portable, host-tested):

- `tr(S::coach_title)`: the message in the UI's language, else English.
  A pointer into flash; no copy, no heap.
- `trn(S::library_tracks_n, n)`: a plural message's form by the
  language's rule (CLDR 46, for whole numbers: `pluralOf(tag, n)`).
- `format(out, size, pattern, args...)`, `trf()`, `trnf()`: `{0}` to
  `{3}` filled into the caller's buffer, cut at a character boundary,
  never past it. An argument is a string, or a number as its digits
  (`Arg(19410)`), `grouped(n)` (the language's thousands mark),
  `decimal(x, places)` (printf's rounding, the language's decimal mark)
  or `fixed(415, 2)` ("4.15"). The arguments are held on the stack, 40
  B each, a few at once. `copy(out, size, text)`: a text as it is, cut
  the same way (the overlays' copies of what they show).
- `setLang()`, `lang()`, `tag()`, `autonym()`, `langOf(tag)`: English
  until the boot sets it from NVS (`app/UiLang`); `hasCatalogue(l)`;
  `translated(id)`, whether the UI's language has its own text for a
  message (the machine translation's note shows only then).
- `line(text, k, ...)`, `lineCount()`: a message's lines.

Numbers as CLDR 46 writes them: "19,410" en, ja, zh; "19.410" de, es,
it, pt; "19 410" fr, pl, ru with a no-break space (French's is U+202F,
which the fonts lack: its look-alike would be a plain space a line may
break at, so U+00A0 stands in); es, it and pl leave four digits alone
("1234": `minimumGroupingDigits` 2). Decimals with "," but in en, ja and
zh. Where English wrote raw digits before ("Play all 19410") it still
does: the code asks for `grouped()` where it grouped.

**The pseudo-locale** `en-XA` (the generator makes it from English: each
letter accented with one the fonts have, a third longer, in brackets:
"[Ţíp {0} öf {1} ĺöŕ]") is compiled where `UISTR_PSEUDO` is defined: the
host tests, or a device build made with it
(`PLATFORMIO_BUILD_FLAGS=-DUISTR_PSEUDO`), whose Language page then lists
it last, as "[Éñĝĺíšĥ]".

### The language setting

**Saved** in NVS `ui`/`lang`, the language's tag ("de", "pt"), written at
once when one is picked (`app/UiLang`; `nvslayout`'s `kLangKey`). The
boot reads it right after the NVS schema's step and before the boot
screen, whose touch hint is the first text drawn: no key, or a tag this
firmware hasn't (a later firmware's language, after a downgrade), is
English. The log says which (`[ui] language: de (Deutsch)`).

**NVS schema 3.** A new key is a layout change by the NVS rules
([ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), "NVS: the rules"), as schema 2's
repeat mode was. The bump also gives the one place that can tell an
update from a fresh unit: `migrate(2, 3)` (and from 1) writes `ui`/`lang`
"en" when there is none, so a unit that ran 0.9.0 or older stays in
English and never sees the first boot's picker. A fresh NVS has no
`schema` key and takes no step, so it has no language: the picker is its.
A downgrade to 0.9.0 finds schema 3 "newer than this firmware's 2", leaves
NVS as it is (it never reads `lang`, and draws English) and keeps every
other setting, as they are all keys it wrote; back on this firmware the
number is current and the language is still there. `test_nvs_layout`'s
migration matrix has each case (a fresh unit, beta.1, 0.6.0, 0.9.0 with
and without the tips seen, a downgrade and back, a picker left without a
choice).

**Output > Language**, after Touch calibration: a globe, "Language", and
under it the language in use in its own name; it opens the Language page
(`PageKind::Language`, one of OutputPage's kinds): the ten in their
order, each in its own name (Bold, with a radio on, for the one in use),
full-width rows hit by y like the touch check's, so a panel that reads x
wrong picks right. The page opens at the language in use. A tap uses it at
once: saved, and the whole UI drawn again in it (`Ui::chooseLanguage()`):
the tab bar, the page (entered again where it was, so what it made of
texts on its way in, the Folders root's "Play all N", is made again),
the tips if they are up; a sheet or dialog up is closed as a tab tap
closes it, and the toast and the HUD go (their texts were the old
language's). Nothing else holds a text: the pages draw from `tr()` each
time, a list's rows are drawn again, the snapshot's texts (the sleep
timer's, the Bluetooth codec line) are made every pass, and the Queue's
summary, which is kept while the queue doesn't change, is made again in
a new language. The one copy is the held track's names while the
library updates (its fence: seconds), which keeps a Various Artists line
in the language it was taken in until then.

A language other than English then shows a toast in itself,
"Machine-translated: expect rough edges" (`language.machine_note`), as
the terminal player does when a language is chosen; only where its
catalogue has that text (`translated()`), so a language without a
catalogue shows none.

**The first boot's picker.** A fresh unit (no language saved and the
tips never seen: `nvslayout::langPickerDue()`, decided in `Ui::begin()`)
starts on the Output tab's Language page with no back arrow and "later:
Output > Language" beside its title. Main.cpp's first-boot touch check,
which on any other unit comes before the UI, waits for it, so the check
speaks the language picked. A pick goes on: Now Playing, the touch check
if it is due (the UI suspended under it), then the tips, and the
language's note over them. Leaving it without a pick (a tab) keeps
English, saves nothing, and goes on the same way; the next boot asks
again only if the tips weren't seen by then. The boot screen's touch
hint, drawn before any of it, is English on a fresh unit.

**The console:** `uiL` lists the languages (a `*` on the one in use, and
which have no catalogue yet), `uiL<tag>` uses one as a tap on its row
does, `uiL-` forgets the language and the tips (the next boot is a fresh
unit's: the picker, the touch check if due, the tips), and `uiP` shows the
first boot's picker now (no tips after it).

### Names the library stores

The library writes one English text into `library.idx`: "Various
Artists", the line a compilation album gets when its tracks say
compilation and none has an album artist (5.4's chain). It stays there in
English (the orders and the dedup of the tracks' own artists compare it,
so they don't change with the UI's language), and the album gets a flag,
`LibraryIndex::kVarious`; `librarytext::albumArtist()`, which every view
of the line goes through (the album rows and header, Now Playing's

Artists" itself is a name, drawn as tagged. Nothing else the library
stores is English: the loose tracks' "(loose tracks)", "Unknown artist"
and the counts are the UI's texts already.

The flag is a change of Stage A's result, so `LibraryIndex::kRulesVersion`
goes 4 to 5 (the file's version stays 6: an older firmware ignores the
bit, but rules 5 is Outdated to it and it rebuilds once). The first boot
of this firmware finds `library.idx` Outdated and rebuilds it once, as
for rules 3 and 4: from the records on a card that has them, about 10 s
at 20,000 tracks (rules 4's measured 10.1 s, rules 3's 9.96 s: no walk,
no scan); by a walk of `/music` on a card with none (a card-reader card
before its scan wrote `tags.bin`), about 9-11 s of walk and 3.5-4 s of
build at 20,000 ([METADATA.md](METADATA.md), 3.2.2's table). A downgrade
rebuilds the same way once.

## Phase 4: right-to-left and complex scripts (not now)

- **Hebrew and Arabic names:** reverse the right-to-left runs and mirror
  brackets, then join Arabic letters with Presentation Forms-B and the
  lam-alef ligatures (about 90 lines for a working demo; Rockbox's
  `bidi.c` is a model). Flash: +65 KB (Hebrew) and +466 KB (Arabic) as VLW,
  3-16 KB at 4 bits and 16 px. Phase 0's composer doesn't compose Arabic
  (its hamza and madda letters): its 8 pairs need the marks' classes and
  a wider mark index in `kPairs`.
- **Thai:** mark-stacking rules; word breaks need a dictionary.
- **Devanagari and the other shaped scripts:** glyph runs shaped on the PC
  (HarfBuzz) by the transfer software; HarfBuzz on the device doesn't fit.
  Their NFC compositions too (the nukta letters, the two-part vowel signs,
  which compose two starters as Hangul's jamo do).
- **A right-to-left UI** (mirrored layout and hit areas): not planned.

## Decisions still open

1. Which scripts and languages first, for the public release.
2. The flash budget for fonts: the app slot is 6 MB with about 4.1 MB used
   since phase 3's translations (3.9 MB after phase 2's store, 2.9 MB
   after phase 1's fonts, 2.5 MB before), and WiFi, HTTP and TLS updates
   need room later (an estimated 0.6-1.0 MB), so about 0.9-1.3 MB is left
   for anything else.
3. Where CJK glyphs come from: built in (phase 2a: any card, 1.05 MB of
   flash), a per-library subset from the transfer software (2b: no flash,
   a contract addition), or both.
4. The Han style of the built-in store: Japanese for now (the generator's
   `--region` makes a Korean, Simplified or Traditional Chinese one).
5. Line heights: decided in phase 1. The rows keep their heights, and
   the few taller glyphs are fitted to the line.
6. The order: one script-aware order for everyone (phase 0's, then 1 and
   2's rank tables), or one that follows the UI language (full UCA with
   tailoring); readings (kana, pinyin) from the transfer for CJK.
7. Contract additions: a glyph-store file, reading fields in `/.mstream`.
   Both producers must agree.
8. Translators: Weblate (shared with mStream's terms?), volunteers, or
   pull requests.
9. Right-to-left at all, for names or the UI.
